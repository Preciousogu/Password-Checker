    Six habits that keep your accounts safe

    A strong password is the start. These habits close the gaps attackers use most.

    Make it long

    Use at least 12 characters, ideally 16 or more. Four random words make a passphrase that is long and easy to remember.

    Use a different password everywhere

    If one site is breached, attackers try the same email and password on banks, email and social media.

    Let a password manager do the work

    It creates and remembers a unique password for every account, so you only need to remember one.

    Turn on two-factor authentication

    An authenticator app or security key stops an attacker even if your password leaks.

    Don't log in from links

    Phishing emails and texts copy real login pages. Type the site's address yourself instead.

    Act quickly after a breach

    When a service you use is breached, change that password, and any others that match it, right away.

    If your password was leaked

    Work through these steps in order. Most accounts can be secured in under an hour.

    1. Change the password now

      Start with your email account, since it can reset every other password.

    2. Replace it everywhere you reused it

      Give each account its own new, unique password.

    3. Turn on two-factor authentication

      Choose an authenticator app or security key over text messages where you can.

    4. Review recent activity

      Look for unfamiliar logins, devices, forwarding rules or payments, and sign out other sessions.

    5. Watch your financial accounts

      Set up alerts with your bank, and consider a credit freeze if personal details were exposed.

    Common questions

    Is it safe to type my password here?

    Yes. The strength check and password generator run entirely in your browser. For the breach check, your password is turned into a hash and only the first 5 characters of that hash are sent to Have I Been Pwned, so your actual password never leaves your device. Nothing is stored.

    How does the breach check work?

    It uses Have I Been Pwned's k-anonymity method. The service returns every leaked hash that starts with the same 5 characters, and your browser looks for a match locally.

    What happens to my email when I check it?

    Your email address is sent to XposedOrNot, a free breach-lookup service, which replies with the breaches it appears in. This site doesn't store it or send it anywhere else.

    Are generated passwords safe to use?

    Yes. They're created on your device using your browser's secure random number generator and are never sent or saved. Store them in a password manager so you don't have to remember them.

    What is entropy?

    Entropy estimates how unpredictable a password is, measured in bits. Each extra bit doubles the number of guesses needed to crack it. Aim for 70 bits or more.

    What makes a password strong?

    Length, randomness and uniqueness. Use 12–16 or more characters, avoid names, dates and keyboard patterns, and never use the same password on two sites.

    Should I change my passwords regularly?

    Not on a fixed schedule. Current guidance is to change a password when there's a reason, such as a breach, and to focus on making each one long and unique.
